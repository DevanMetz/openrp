OPENRP.
PUBLIC ALPHA · DESKTOP BROWSER

A city worth playing in.

OpenRP is a free, open-source multiplayer roleplay sandbox inspired by DarkRP. It is an independent project, unaffiliated with Garry’s Mod or Facepunch. Bring a keyboard, a mouse, and a story.

Community rules

  1. Give conflict a roleplay reason. No random killing, spawn camping, or repeatedly targeting a player to ruin their session.
  2. Build fairly. Don’t block public roads, spawns, or other players’ exits. No trapping people with props or using props to bypass fights.
  3. Respect the people behind the characters. No harassment, hate speech, threats, doxxing, or sexual content. Keep personal information out of chat and character names.
  4. Play without exploits. No cheats, connection spam, impersonating operators, or creating identities to manipulate money or elections.

Operators can remove or ban identities that break these rules. City laws chosen by the mayor are part of roleplay; they cannot override community rules.

What to expect in the alpha

Jobs, shops, property, physics building, printers, elections, combat and arrests work in a shared city, with optional proximity voice. There is no fixed player slot cap. Performance depends on server load. Vehicles and Source-engine assets are not included.

Your wallet, inventory, ammunition, props, shops and property return with the same browser identity, including after server updates. Props stay in the shared world while you are offline. Jobs resume when a slot is available, and normal gameplay or operator cleanup can still remove items. Updates can briefly interrupt play. Clearing browser data loses your anonymous identity; there is no account recovery yet.

Privacy

No email, password, or payment is needed. Your browser stores a randomly generated reconnect credential, name and settings. The server stores your character, inventory, wallet, owned world objects, an internal ID and a hash of that credential so you can return after a refresh or update. Bans store an identity ID and operator reason.

Text chat is logged for 30 days for moderation and troubleshooting. This includes nearby, OOC, advert, action and group channels. Logs contain the message, time, public character ID, name, job and channel. Operators can search these logs; they are not publicly accessible. Group chat is visible to operators. Keep personal information and passwords out of all chat channels.

First-party analytics count visits to the home page, joins, anonymous returning identities, connected playtime, jobs, in-game purchases, deaths and moderation actions. The game also records server performance and traffic totals, including voice traffic counts. Referrers retain only the source hostname, not the full URL. These analytics and text logs are automatically removed after 30 UTC calendar days. They do not contain raw browser credentials, IP addresses or voice audio.

Proximity voice starts with the microphone off. Enable it explicitly, allow the browser permission, then hold V while playing to speak to players within 28 metres. Release V to stop. Menus, text chat, leaving the tab and respawning stop transmission. Turn the microphone off in Settings to release the device. Mute individual residents in Tab, or mute all voice in Settings. These community rules also apply to voice; do not harass people or blast disruptive audio.

Voice audio passes through the game server and its hosting/network providers and is relayed only to eligible nearby listeners. The game does not record or transcribe it. HTTPS encrypts transport; this is not end-to-end encryption. Other players may independently record conversations. Individual mute preferences are stored in your browser. Avoid sharing private information in voice chat.

Hosting and DNS providers (Railway and Cloudflare) process network requests and may keep operational logs including IP addresses. The game uses source IPs temporarily to limit connection spam. There are no advertising trackers or third-party game assets.

For a bug report or data request, use the project’s issue tracker. Share your public roleplay name and approximate time, never your browser credential or sensitive personal information. Use GitHub’s private vulnerability reporting for security issues when available.
